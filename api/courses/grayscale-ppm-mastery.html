// Know the house before you walk in

Grayscale: the firm that forced the Bitcoin ETF open.

Founded in 2013, Grayscale is the largest crypto-focused asset manager in the world, roughly $35B AUM as of late 2025, with 40+ products and one of the longest track records in the space. Its identity is built on a single defining act: it sued the SEC, won unanimously, and converted GBTC into a spot Bitcoin ETF in January 2024 — opening the entire US market. Everything below is current as of early 2026; verify any specific number live before you cite it, since AUM and product details move.

Note: figures are point-in-time from public filings/press. Treat them as directional context, not talking-point precision. Don't lead with a number you haven't re-checked the week of the onsite.

POSITION

Pioneer, now challenged

First mover and brand leader, but now competing with giants like BlackRock whose spot ETFs undercut it on fee. The strategic tension: defend the franchise while out-innovating on breadth.

BUSINESS MODEL

Management fees on AUM

Revenue is fee-based on assets, not transaction-based — steadier than an exchange, but it rises and falls with crypto prices and flows. Revenue dipped ~20% in the first nine months of 2025.

OWNERSHIP

DCG-controlled, eyeing public

Subsidiary of Barry Silbert's Digital Currency Group. Filed an S-1 to list on NYSE as "GRAY" (Up-C, dual-class; DCG keeps control). The listing has been paced against market conditions.

Leadership & culture signals

CEO Peter Mintzberg (since Aug 2024) came from Goldman Sachs (Global Head of Strategy, AWM), with prior strategy/M&A roles at BlackRock, Apollo, Invesco, and a McKinsey start. He's a TradFi-strategy operator deliberately bringing institutional discipline to a crypto-native firm — the same bridge you'd be hired to build on the product side.
Founder/chairman Barry Silbert runs parent DCG. Founders-with-conviction culture: the SEC lawsuit was a bet-the-reputation move that defined the brand.
Research-forward identity. Grayscale publishes serious market research (e.g. the "~5% allocation" thesis) and frames itself as institutional-grade access, not a retail trading venue. Stamford, CT HQ; deep TradFi pedigree on the leadership bench.
The moment you're joining. Maturing from "the GBTC company" into a diversified, multi-product, soon-to-be-public asset manager — exactly why an AI-workflow/operating-discipline Principal PM mandate exists right now.

How to use this in the room

Connect your pitch to their inflection. They're scaling product breadth and heading toward public-company operating rigor. Your founder + Stellar data-platform background maps directly onto "establish product operating discipline and AI-driven workflows."

Show you respect the regulated posture. The deterministic-foundation, audit-first instinct from your other prep is exactly the maturity a soon-to-be-public, SEC-registered asset manager needs.

Don't overclaim crypto-nativeness. Mintzberg himself is a TradFi operator who took time to learn the space. Your honest "I bring product and platform discipline and I'm deep on the architecture" is the right register — it mirrors how their own CEO is positioned.

Ask sharp company questions. How does the public-listing timeline change product operating requirements? Where does the org sit today on project-execution vs product-ownership? Which workflow is the most painful to run manually right now?

// How to use this week

Learn the domain, then drill until it's reflex.

Tabs 1–5 are the knowledge layer: read them like a textbook, expand every panel. Tabs 6–10 are the reinforcement layer: flashcards, a scored quiz with a streak, sequencing puzzles, trade-off drills, and conflict scenarios. The pattern that works: read a domain in the morning, drill it the same evening, then re-drill cold the next day. Active recall beats re-reading every time. Aim to get the Mastery Quiz to 18+/22 by Friday and to clear every conflict scenario without peeking.

The five things to be able to do cold by Friday

Explain NAV, creation/redemption, custody, and why a digital-asset fund's operational risk differs from a traditional one — in plain language, to a non-technical exec.
Whiteboard an event-driven ingest-to-ledger pipeline and defend every component, every failure mode, and every build-vs-buy call.
Pick and apply the right prioritization framework on the spot, and articulate the difference between sequencing and priority.
Describe how you'd move a team from project execution to product ownership in 90 days, with concrete operating mechanics.
Navigate a stakeholder or direct-report conflict toward an outcome without either steamrolling or caving.
